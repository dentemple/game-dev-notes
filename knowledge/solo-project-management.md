# Solo Project Management & Shipping

## Three artifacts (primary)

- "one prioritized backlog (everything goes in it, most of it dies in it)"
- "a current milestone defined as a playable slice ('demo a stranger can finish')"
- "a weekly fifteen-minute review where you re-aim"
Source: https://bugnet.io/blog/project-management-for-solo-game-devs (accessed 2026-11-18).

## Scope discipline (primary)

- "cut scope — not quality, not the date — when it slips."
- Milestones must be runnable and showable: "'Finish combat system' is unfalsifiable; 'a stranger can play three encounters and tell me if it's fun' is a milestone." Define each milestone "2-6 weeks out."
- De-risking sequence: core loop → vertical slice → content breadth → polish.
Source: https://bugnet.io/blog/project-management-for-solo-game-devs (accessed 2026-11-18).

## Motivation & burnout (practitioner)

- "Track only two metrics: milestone date confidence (gut, but written down) and weeks of motivation runway." If either trends down two reviews running, the answer is "scope surgery, not harder work."
- "Progress you can see is fuel" — ship small visible wins; "shipping anything, even a menu fix, restarts the engine."
- Roughly 90% of solo projects fail to complete in three years, usually from constant rework of core mechanics/narrative/art. Manage expectations, keep financial runway, and get external feedback early.
Source: https://bugnet.io/blog/project-management-for-solo-game-devs and https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/how-to-make-a-game-alone-2 (accessed 2026-11-18).

## Note for solo dev

Scope is the one variable a solo developer fully controls — not speed, not team size. Small and shipped beats large and unfinished. Treat every new feature as a cost center (it drags UI, animation, audio, balance, save data, and QA behind it).
