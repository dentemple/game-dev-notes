# Design Best Practices (Onboarding, Playtesting, Difficulty)

## Onboarding (primary)

- "Most games lose their largest single chunk of players within the first 10 minutes of play."
- "Players don't want to learn your game. They want to play it." — "the best FTUEs teach mechanics by making players use them immediately."
- "This is documentation, not onboarding." — don't front-load text walls.
Source: https://go.vgm.co/blog/ftue-design-mistakes-that-kill-games-before-players-hit-level-2 (accessed 2026-11-18).

## Onboarding patterns (practitioner consensus)

- First 60-90 seconds: the player touches controls and performs one satisfying action (no menus/logos/character-creation first).
- First 10 minutes: core loop runs 3-5 times, one verb practiced (not all at once).
- One concept per teaching moment; progressive disclosure; safe, recoverable failure; always allow skipping.
- Teach contextually (show, don't tell) — Half-Life 2's "Pick up that can", Breath of the Wild's Great Plateau.
Source: gamesindustry.biz + helpshift + design references (accessed 2026-11-18; RESEARCH).

## Playtesting & iteration (practitioner consensus)

- "Test early, test often, iterate constantly" — fun cannot be planned in advance, only discovered.
- Build a minimal playable prototype and test it with strangers; define a specific testable question, not just "is this fun?"
- 1-on-1 synchronous playtesting reveals confusion and emotion live; late playtesting is far less valuable (changes cost weeks, not hours).
- Bias risk: friends/family and internal testers skew positive — use external players early.
Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-you-yes-you-can-benefit-from-1-on-1-synchronous-playtesting + Valve GDC 2006 (accessed 2026-11-18; RESEARCH).

## Difficulty & flow (practitioner consensus)

- Ramp difficulty around mastery; keep controls legible; failure should be attributable and recoverable (player should know why they failed and how to fix it).
- Watch metrics: day-1 retention, tutorial completion rate, time-to-core-loop.

## Note for solo dev

Playtest a rough prototype weekly; the cheapest moment to learn your game is wrong is before investing in art and content.
